## Overall Impression

This is a disciplined, well-reasoned implementation of its own design system — the code repeatedly shows domain awareness (phone-first bookings, real schedule validation, Buenos-Aires time handling) that goes beyond what a template would produce. The gap isn't craft, it's two structural choices made in service of the monochrome/disclosure rules: the outbound-link disclosure pattern has a real bypass, and the booking form asks a first-time, trust-uncertain visitor to make a 6-way service decision and pick a date with no visible signal of which days are closed, right at the highest-stakes moment on the page.

## What's Working

1. **`selectedService` continuity** (`App.jsx`, `Services.jsx`, `Booking.jsx`) — picking a service in "La carta" pre-fills the booking form's dropdown. A concrete working-memory win, not just a nice-to-have.
2. **Inline validation copy** (`Booking.jsx` `validate()`) — messages like "Ese día cerramos, elegí otro" are specific and instructive rather than generic "invalid field" text.
3. **Focus-trap/`inert` discipline**, applied consistently in two independent places (mobile nav panel, gallery lightbox): background content is properly non-tabbable while covered, and focus returns to the trigger on close.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Closed-day information arrives after the user has already tried to book.**
Why it matters: `Booking.jsx`'s two-column layout only splits at the `lg:` breakpoint, so on everything below 1024px — the primary context per DESIGN.md's own mobile-first framing — the "Horario" schedule renders after the form in scroll order. A user filling in a date has no way to know Mondays/Sundays are closed until they submit and get an error, which is an avoidable stumble at the page's single highest-stakes moment.
Fix: surface a one-line "Cerrado lunes y domingos" note directly above the date field, not just in the error message after the fact.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Outbound-link disclosure only fires on primary click, not on the other ways a link can be actioned.**
Why it matters: every outbound link (Instagram, Maps, phone, footer legal) uses `href="#"` + `preventDefault()` inside `onClick`. Right-click → "copy link address," right-click → "open in new tab," and middle-click all bypass the handler and either do nothing or go to `#` with zero disclosure — directly undercutting PRODUCT.md's own stated principle of "disclosure over illusion."
Fix: either disclose via the link's visible/aria state (so it's true regardless of interaction path) or accept `#` as an intentionally inert target and make that legible some other way; don't rely solely on `onClick`.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] "La carta" and the booking form both present an unscaffolded 6-way decision with no subgrouping.**
Why it matters: six services with only a one-line description and a single "Más pedido" badge is a decision point above the 4-item working-memory guideline, and the "Turno" fieldset then bundles service + barber + date + time + notes as one flat 5-field group right before the same decision repeats in the form's own dropdown.
Fix: give the Turno fieldset internal grouping (what vs. when), and let the "Más pedido" signal (or a short "first time? start here" cue) do more decision-support work in La carta.
Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`

**[P3] No reassurance near the booking commitment point.**
Why it matters: no cancellation note, no explicit response-time commitment beyond "te escribimos en un rato," and the trust signals established in About (years in business, rating) never resurface near the form — a gap for a persona explicitly deciding whether to trust an unfamiliar shop.
Fix: re-surface one or two trust signals, and add a one-line cancellation/response-time note, near the submit button.
Suggested command: `/impeccable onboard`

**[P3] Tonal tension between "not a trendy place" copy and an unbroken high-polish monochrome register.**
Why it matters: About's copy explicitly disclaims trendiness ("No somos un lugar de moda: somos la barbería a la que volvés") while every photo across all eight sections is `grayscale contrast-125` with zero warmth exceptions — coherent and well-executed, but worth naming as a positioning tension between words and register.
Fix: consider one deliberate warmth exception (a candid, less-posed shot) near the trust-building sections, or lean into the tension explicitly rather than leaving it unaddressed.
Suggested command: `/impeccable critique` (revisit after other fixes, to judge whether the tension still reads as a problem)

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer)**: Picks "Corte + Barba" off the "Más pedido" badge — this works well. On mobile, fills the form and might pick a Monday without knowing the shop is closed (schedule sits below the form — see P1), getting an error only after submitting. Never encounters first-visit guidance (walk-in fallback, cancellation terms); the appointment-only policy is mentioned once in About, three sections upstream, easy to have skimmed past.

**Riley (Deliberate Stress Tester)**: Submitting the form empty, or with a past time on today's date, is correctly caught with clear messaging — this holds up well. But right-clicking "Ver en Google Maps," the phone number, or an Instagram link to "copy link address" or "open in new tab" produces `#` with no toast and no explanation — the one place Riley would flag the demo as actually deceptive rather than transparently disclosed, directly against the product's own stated principle.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: Benefits from `MobileBar` staying pinned with "Sacar turno"/"Llamar" once she scrolls past Hero. But tapping "Llamar" — the single most natural mobile expectation for a "Call" button — produces a demo-disclosure toast instead of opening the dialer. That's the correct product trade-off given PRODUCT.md's constraints, but it's real friction for exactly the persona most likely to tap it reflexively, and she hits the same closed-day submission failure as Jordan, magnified by one-handed scrolling past the schedule info below the form.

## Questions to Consider

- Is "unpretentious neighborhood barbershop" copy actually compatible with an unbroken, agency-portfolio-grade monochrome system for eight straight sections, or does the visual polish undercut the warmth the copy claims?
- DESIGN.md declares block inversion the only emphasis mechanism, and it's now used for: CTAs, hover states, active nav, form errors, the Instagram card, and the marquee. Does one signifier doing six different jobs still reliably tell a user what kind of thing they're looking at?
- Should a booking-intent landing page for a local service business always budget space for cancellation/first-visit reassurance, or is that legitimately out of scope for what this page is trying to be?
